Follow-up to X004: is the seed already in Stampedes II, and does its ODE have a physical meaning? An exact auxiliary Coulomb interpretation and its limits; no new physical result is claimed.

1. Is it already in Stampedes II? The corrected Kummer seed and its ODE were not found there. Page 1, Eq.(2) and the following paragraph give I0(2s), the stricter highest-log limit. It drops the inverse-log corrections retained in X004. The relevant later author papers and citing literature examined did not reveal an explicit equivalent closed seed. General determinants, bridge resolvents and the auxiliary differential equation were already published: the answer is partly implicit prior art. See [PRIOR_ART.md](PRIOR_ART.md) for locations and search limits.

2. Does the ODE have physical meaning? Exactly: it is the zero-angular-momentum radial Coulomb scattering equation in two dimensions, with L as radius, B as charge and energy pi^2. The bridge ratio is a dressed boundary mode of the existing Bessel determinant. This supplies auxiliary scattering intuition; it does not derive a new Coulomb particle in SYM. See the one-page [PHYSICS.md](PHYSICS.md).

The interpretation gives a logarithmic Coulomb phase and proves that increasing B delays every positive zero. Its double-scaling limit recovers Stampedes II’s Bessel seed; its local expansion agrees with the printed corrections. The papers provide no large-L zero data, so those consequences have not been tested on new physical data or established for the full finite-cross-ratio octagon.

The independent [referee](REFEREE.md) agrees with the algebra and these limits. A direct probability interpretation, a microscopic spin-chain identification and a new Painleve/Toda mechanism were not established. Vieira’s Complex Spin paper already contains a different hydrogen analogy.

NEW: no for this follow-up. These are explanatory consequences of the existing seed, not a new physical discovery. The earlier resummation claim is not withdrawn; its explicit corrected closed form was not found in Stampedes II. The exact determinant remains the general standard method.
